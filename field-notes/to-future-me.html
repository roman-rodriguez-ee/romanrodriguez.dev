At the Beginning of the Road

I am writing this at the beginning of my engineering career, before the title, before the long-term clarity, and before I fully know where this road is going to take me.

Right now, I am still building the foundation. I am learning how to think like an engineer, how to ask better questions, how to document my work, and how to turn confusion into progress. Some days feel exciting. Some days feel frustrating. There are times when I feel behind, and there are times when I can see how much progress I have already made.

What excites me most is seeing my “crazy” ideas come to life. What’s the worst thing people can say? No? When they say something can’t work, ask “why not?” I love working with people who are way smarter than me and do not make me feel stupid. It is okay to not know everything because the fun part is learning something new. I do not just want to earn a degree or get a good job. I want to become the type of engineer and man people can trust when the work matters.

My biggest struggle right now is balancing everything: school, internship work, personal growth, finances, family, and trying to build a future that feels bigger than where I started. Coming from an environment with zero exposure to the engineering world, I have serious imposter syndrome at my first internship. I have to tell myself, “You just have to start somewhere.” Failure is inevitable. Learning from it is what matters most.

If I am reading this years from now in a senior engineering role, I hope I remember this version of myself. I hope I remember what it felt like to be new, to be unsure, and to have to earn confidence one project at a time.

I hope I lead people with that memory in mind.

I hope I never become the type of leader who forgets what it felt like to be learning. I hope I explain the mission clearly, give people room to grow, hold high standards, and take responsibility when things get difficult.

Right now, I do not know exactly where I will end up. Defense, hardware, embedded systems, RF communication, robotics, energy, medical devices, or something I have not even discovered yet are all still possible. But I know the direction I want to move: toward technical depth, leadership, ownership, and work that matters.

Future me, remember this stage.

Remember the uncertainty.

Remember the hunger.

Remember the people who helped you.

Remember that confidence was built slowly.

And if you ever get comfortable, find the next hard thing and grow again.

— Roman in 2026
